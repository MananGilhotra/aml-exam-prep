▷ Central Question

Hum kaise jaanein ki ek trained model actually accha hai? Ek useful metric ko us kind and cost of error se match karna chahiye jo us decision ke liye matter karta hai.

⚡ Ek Line Mein

Regression mein errors ka size hota hai: MAE (linear, original units), MAPE (relative %), MSE/RMSE (large errors ko punish), $R^2$ (mean baseline se kitna behtar), Adjusted $R^2$ (extra predictors ko charge karta hai). 3Regression Error Metrics

▷ Central Question

Signed residuals direction dikhate hain, par positive aur negative values cancel ho jaate hain (upar dekho: signed total sirf $-4$ hai jabki individual errors 5 tak ja rahe hain). Saare aath errors ke size ko kaise summarise karein?

3.1 Mean Absolute Error (MAE)

QuestionOriginal target unit mein typical absolute miss kitna hai?
Formula & result$\text{MAE} = \dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|e_{A,i}| = \dfrac{22}{8} = \mathbf{2.75}$ thousand INR
AdvantageInterpret karna easy; har extra unit of error ko equal linear weight milta hai.
DisadvantagesDirection hide karta hai, target scale par depend karta hai, aur ek rare large miss ko emphasise nahi karta.

3.2 Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)

QuestionActual value ke relative typical error kitna bada hai?
Formula & result$\text{MAPE} = \dfrac{100}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\dfrac{e_{A,i}}{y_i}\right| = \dfrac{45.50\%}{8} \approx \mathbf{5.69\%}$
AdvantageEk scale-free percentage jo tab convenient hai jab relative error matter karta hai.
Disadvantages$y_i = 0$ par undefined, zero ke paas unstable, aur chhoti actual values ko over-emphasise kar sakta hai.

3.3 Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)

MetricFormula and Model A resultAdvantageDisadvantage
MSE $\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{A,i}^2 = \dfrac{74}{8} = \mathbf{9.25}$
(thousand INR)$^2$		 Large misses ko strongly penalise karta hai; optimisation mein useful. Squared units; unusual large errors ke prati sensitive.
RMSE $\sqrt{\text{MSE}} = \sqrt{9.25} \approx \mathbf{3.04}$
thousand INR		 Large-error penalty rakhta hai par target unit mein wapas le aata hai. Abhi bhi scale-dependent aur MAE se zyada outlier-sensitive.

  1. Concept check: if one absolute residual rises from 1 to 5, its contribution to $\sum|e_i|$ rises by ______, while its contribution to $\sum e_i^2$ rises by ______. Which metric family reacts more strongly? $$\text{Absolute: } 5 - 1 = \mathbf{4}\ \text{increase}$$ $$\text{Squared: } 5^2 - 1^2 = 25 - 1 = \mathbf{24}\ \text{increase}$$
    The squared family (MSE / RMSE) reacts far more strongly — 6× the increase for the same change in residual.
  2. True or false: "MSE = 9.25 means a typical miss of 9.25 thousand INR."
    False.

    MSE ke units squared hain — (thousand INR)$^2$. Typical miss RMSE $= \sqrt{9.25} \approx 3.04$ thousand INR hai. Yeh sabse common interpretation error hai.

Verify all four totals from the master table

  1. $\sum|e_A| = 22$ $$2+1+3+3+1+3+4+5 = 22\ \checkmark \qquad \Rightarrow \text{MAE} = 22/8 = 2.75$$
  2. $\sum e_A^2 = 74$ $$4+1+9+9+1+9+16+25 = 74\ \checkmark \qquad \Rightarrow \text{MSE} = 74/8 = 9.25,\ \ \text{RMSE} = 3.04$$
  3. $\sum\text{APE}_A \approx 45.50\%$ $$6.667+2.857+7.500+6.667+2.000+5.455+6.667+7.692 = 45.504\ \checkmark \Rightarrow \text{MAPE} = 5.69\%$$
  4. $\sum e_B^2 = 60$ $$4+1+9+4+1+9+16+16 = 60\ \checkmark$$

    Model B sirf S4 ($42 \to 43$) aur S8 ($70 \to 69$) par alag hai; dono cases mein wo closer hai, isliye $74 \to 60$.

  5. (c) When would you report MAE, MAPE, or RMSE? And what is MAPE's zero-value limitation?

    MAE — jab har unit of error same cost rakhta ho, aur jab tumhe non-technical stakeholders ko ek interpretable number dena ho ("on average we are off by 2.75k").
    MAPE — jab relative error matter kare aur alag scales ke models compare karne hon (₹30k stipend par ₹3k error ka matlab ₹6 lakh salary par ₹3k error se bohot alag hai).
    RMSE — jab ek bada miss bohot saare chhote misses se zyada costly ho.
    MAPE's zero limitation: formula mein $y_i$ denominator mein hai. Agar koi actual value $0$ hai, to APE undefined ho jaata hai (division by zero); aur agar $y_i$ zero ke bohot paas hai to APE explode kar jaata hai aur poora average dominate kar leta hai.

4$R^2$ from the Mean-Only Baseline ⭐

▷ Central Question

Pichle chaar metrics batate hain ki residuals kitne bade hain. Par kya Model A us model se meaningfully behtar hai jo har student ko same stipend predict kar deta?

Full computation

Computing $R^2$ for Model A step by step

  1. Step 1 — Build the mean-only baseline $$\bar y = \frac{30+35+40+45+50+55+60+65}{8} = \frac{380}{8} = \mathbf{47.5}$$

    The baseline predicts 47.5 thousand INR for every student, regardless of input.

  2. Step 2 — Measure the total variation available to explain (TSS) $$\text{TSS} = \sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i - \bar y)^2$$
    $y_i$3035404550556065
    $y_i - \bar y$−17.5−12.5−7.5−2.52.57.512.517.5
    $(y_i-\bar y)^2$306.25156.2556.256.256.2556.25156.25306.25
    $$\text{TSS} = 306.25+156.25+56.25+6.25+6.25+56.25+156.25+306.25 = \mathbf{1050}$$
  3. Step 3 — Measure the variation left after using Model A (RSS) $$\text{RSS} = \sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i - \hat y_i)^2 = \mathbf{74}$$

    RSS residual sum of squares hai: wo squared variation jo Model A ke baad bhi unexplained bachi hai.

  4. Step 4 — Compare leftover variation with original variation $$R^2 = 1 - \frac{\text{RSS}}{\text{TSS}} = 1 - \frac{74}{1050} = 1 - 0.07048 = \mathbf{0.9295}$$

    Equivalent explained-fraction view:

    $$\frac{\text{TSS} - \text{RSS}}{\text{TSS}} = \frac{1050 - 74}{1050} = \frac{976}{1050} = \mathbf{0.9295}\ \checkmark$$
★ Key Insight

TSS actual stipends ka unke mean ke around total squared variation hai. Yeh wo variation hai jise trained model account karne ka mauka rakhta hai. Agar hum sirf mean predict karein, to poori 1050 unexplained reh jaati hai.

In students par, Model A mean-only baseline ke around maujood squared variation ka lagbhag 92.95% account karta hai. Lagbhag 7.05% unexplained reh jaata hai.

Interpret the landmarks

ValueCorrect interpretation
$R^2 = 1$Perfect fit: predictions har actual value se match karti hain aur $\text{RSS} = 0$.
$R^2 = 0$No improvement over the mean-only baseline: $\text{RSS} = \text{TSS}$.
$0 < R^2 < 1$Mean-only baseline se better, par kuch variation abhi bhi unexplained hai.
$R^2 < 0$Worse than the mean-only baseline on the evaluated data: $\text{RSS} > \text{TSS}$.
△ DO NOT LET THIS MISCONCEPTION SURVIVE

$R^2 = 0.93$ ka matlab 93% accuracy NAHI hai, na hi "93% of predictions are correct", aur na hi "7% average error". $R^2$ ek relative sum-of-squares comparison hai mean baseline ke saath. Error size ke liye MAE ya RMSE report karo.

Practice P3

Same RMSE, different $R^2$

Q: Do datasets ka same RMSE ho sakta hai par different $R^2$ values. Konsi additional property $R^2$ badalti hai?

The spread (variance) of the target $y$ around its mean — i.e. TSS.

Reasoning: $R^2 = 1 - \frac{\text{RSS}}{\text{TSS}}$ aur $\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\text{RSS}/n}$. Same RMSE (same $n$) ⟹ same RSS. Par $R^2$ TSS par bhi depend karta hai.

Concrete example: Dono datasets mein $\text{RSS} = 74$ aur $n = 8$, to dono ka RMSE $= 3.04$.

  • Dataset 1: stipends 30–65 range mein, $\text{TSS} = 1050$ ⟹ $R^2 = 1 - 74/1050 = \mathbf{0.93}$ — excellent.
  • Dataset 2: stipends sab 47–48 ke paas, $\text{TSS} = 80$ ⟹ $R^2 = 1 - 74/80 = \mathbf{0.075}$ — almost useless.

Intuition: agar sabka stipend lagbhag same hai, to mean baseline already bohot accha hai — usse behtar karna mushkil hai. Agar stipends bohot spread hain, to mean baseline weak hai aur usse behtar karna easy hai. $R^2$ tumhari baseline ke difficulty par depend karta hai, sirf tumhare error par nahi.

5$R^2$ Limitations and Adjusted $R^2$ ⭐

▷ Central Question

Agar ek student ke roll number ka last digit add karne se training $R^2$ thoda badh jaye, to kya us extra predictor ne model mein apni jagah earn kar li?

Ordinary least squares mein, training data par intercept ke saath, ek predictor add karne se RSS badh nahi sakti; isliye training $R^2$ ghat nahi sakta. Ek useless feature bhi noise fit kar sakta hai. $R^2$ fit ko reward karta hai par complexity ko charge nahi karta.

Adjusted $R^2$$$R^2_{\text{adj}} = 1 - (1 - R^2)\,\frac{n-1}{n-p-1} \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad R^2_{\text{adj}} = 1 - \left(\frac{\text{RSS}}{\text{TSS}}\right)\left(\frac{n-1}{n-p-1}\right)$$

where $n$ = number of observations and $p$ = number of predictors, excluding the intercept. Factor $\frac{n-1}{n-p-1}$ $p$ ke saath badhta hai, to ek naye predictor ko unexplained variation itni kam karni padegi ki wo badi penalty ko overcome kar sake.

★ Adjusted $R^2$ — term by term
  • $R^2$ model ka mean-only baseline ke upar raw improvement hai; $1 - R^2$ wo fraction hai jo unexplained bacha hai.
  • $n$ observations ki sankhya hai. Numerator $n-1$ target mean estimate karne se tied hai.
  • $p$ predictors ki sankhya hai, intercept excluding; $n - p - 1$ residual degrees of freedom hain.
  • Ratio $\frac{n-1}{n-p-1}$ unexplained fraction ko enlarge karta hai jaise predictors add hote hain. Bahar ka $1-$ use wapas ek adjusted explained-variation score mein convert kar deta hai.
Worked Comparison

Did the extra feature earn its place?

Model B predicts $(32, 34, 43, 43, 49, 58, 56, 69)$ for S1–S8; its extra predictor reduces RSS from 74 to 60. Here $n = 8$.

ModelPredictors$p$RSS$R^2$Adjusted $R^2$
ACGPA + interview score2740.92950.9013
BModel A + roll-number last digit3600.94290.9000
  1. Model A — Adjusted $R^2$ $$R^2_{\text{adj},A} = 1 - \left(\frac{74}{1050}\right)\left(\frac{8-1}{8-2-1}\right) = 1 - (0.070476)\left(\frac{7}{5}\right)$$ $$= 1 - (0.070476)(1.4) = 1 - 0.098667 = \mathbf{0.9013}$$
  2. Model B — Adjusted $R^2$ $$R^2_{\text{adj},B} = 1 - \left(\frac{60}{1050}\right)\left(\frac{8-1}{8-3-1}\right) = 1 - (0.057143)\left(\frac{7}{4}\right)$$ $$= 1 - (0.057143)(1.75) = 1 - 0.100000 = \mathbf{0.9000}$$
  3. Also compute the raw $R^2$ values for comparison $$R^2_A = 1 - \frac{74}{1050} = \mathbf{0.9295}, \qquad R^2_B = 1 - \frac{60}{1050} = \mathbf{0.9429}$$
  4. Decision
    Model B has the higher raw $R^2$ (0.9429), but Model A has the higher Adjusted $R^2$ (0.9013 > 0.9000).

    The roll-number feature did NOT earn its place. Model B ne RSS kam ki (74 → 60) aur raw $R^2$ badha, par improvement itna chhota tha ki teesre-predictor ki penalty offset nahi kar paaya. Penalty factor $1.4 \to 1.75$ jump kar gaya — ek 25% badhi hui penalty, jabki unexplained fraction sirf $0.0705 \to 0.0571$ (19% reduction) hua.

    Note: yahan $1-R^2 = \text{RSS}/\text{TSS}$ use karne se rounded $R^2$ values carry karne ki zaroorat nahi padti — zyada accurate hai.

★ What Adjusted $R^2$ does — and does NOT — tell us
  • Same target aur observations par fit kiye gaye, par different $p$ wale regression models compare karne ke liye useful.
  • Jab ek naya feature bohot kam explanatory value add karta hai to yeh ghat sakta hai; normally $R^2_{\text{adj}} \le R^2$.
  • Automatic feature-selection oracle nahi hai, aur validation ya cross-validation ka replacement nahi hai.
  • $n > p+1$ chahiye, aur $p$ mein intercept count nahi hota.
♦ Takeaway — the Part 1 ladder

Signed residual → absolute size → relative size → large-error penalty → target units → mean baseline → feature-count penalty.

★ Why accuracy is not enough

Accuracy har wrong label ko ek error ginti hai, par wo us error ka type ya direction identify nahi karti. Do bilkul alag mistakes consider karo:

  • Error Type 1 — "Missed Ready": ek student jo actually Ready hai par model Needs Practice ya Needs Support predict karta hai. Student capable tha, phir bhi model use recognise nahi kar paaya.
  • Error Type 2 — "False Ready": ek student jo actually Needs Practice ya Needs Support hai par model Ready predict karta hai. Student ko help chahiye thi, par model ne use ready label kar diya.

Dono error types accuracy ko exactly ek count se kam karte hain. Accuracy score akela in bilkul alag kism ki incorrect predictions mein farak nahi kar sakta. Yeh nahi bata sakta ki model primarily help-needing students ko miss kar raha hai, ya struggling students ko galti se ready label kar raha hai. Kis kism ki galtiyan ho rahi hain aur kaunsi classes confuse ho rahi hain, yeh samajhne ke liye confusion matrix, precision aur recall chahiye.

Metric map

MetricFormulaMeaning for Needs Support
Precision$\dfrac{TP}{TP + FP}$Predicted Support alerts mein se, kitne correct hain?
Recall / TPR$\dfrac{TP}{TP + FN}$Actual Support students mein se, kitne found hue?

★ Keep the denominators straight
  • Precision uses predicted positives: $TP + FP$.  (FP ↑ ⟹ Precision ↓ — Type I error)
  • Recall / TPR uses actual positives: $TP + FN$.  (FN ↑ ⟹ Recall ↓ — Type II error)
  • F1 balances precision and recall and does not use TN. TN abhi bhi binary confusion view ko complete karta hai.

△ Undefined cases

Agar ek model kisi class ko kabhi predict hi nahi karta, to uska precision denominator zero ho jaata hai. Agar evaluation set mein kisi class ka koi actual example hi nahi hai, to uska recall denominator zero ho jaata hai. Ek report mein state karna chahiye ki library ne undefined value ko kaise handle kiya (usually 0 set karke ya warning deke).

Practice P9

What changes when one miss is corrected?

Suppose S15 changes from Predicted Practice to Predicted Support. Updated counts: $TP = 3$, $FP = 2$, $FN = 1$, $TN = 12$.

$$\text{Precision}_S = \frac{3}{3+2} = \frac{3}{5} = \mathbf{0.600}$$ $$\text{Recall}_S = \frac{3}{3+1} = \frac{3}{4} = \mathbf{0.750}$$ $$F1_S = \frac{2(3)}{2(3)+2+1} = \frac{6}{9} = \mathbf{0.667}$$

Notice: ek hi correction ne recall ko $0.500 \to 0.750$ (+50%) uthaya par precision sirf $0.500 \to 0.600$ — kyunki FP unchanged raha. Ek FN ko TP mein badalna dono ratios improve karta hai, par recall ko zyada.

Cost scenarioPrioritiseWhy
(a) Missing Needs Support students is costliestRecallMissing = FN. Recall ka denominator ($TP+FN$) hi FN ko punish karta hai.
(b) Unnecessary Support alerts are costliestPrecisionFalse alerts = FP. Precision ka denominator ($TP+FP$) FP ko punish karta hai.

9Macro, Weighted, and Micro Averages ⭐

▷ Central Question

Accuracy ek overall count deti hai. Jab precision, recall ya F1 ko several classes par summarise karna ho — to kya har class ko equal weight milna chahiye, ya bade classes ko zyada contribute karna chahiye?

Where these per-class numbers come from (verify them yourself)

Ready: Precision $= 6/7 = 0.857$ (column R total is 7, of which 6 are truly R). Recall $= 6/8 = 0.750$ (row R total is 8). F1 $= \frac{2(0.857)(0.750)}{0.857+0.750} = 0.800$.

Needs Practice: Precision $= 4/7 = 0.571$. Recall $= 4/6 = 0.667$. F1 $= \frac{2(0.571)(0.667)}{0.571+0.667} = 0.615$.

Needs Support: Precision $= 2/4 = 0.500$. Recall $= 2/4 = 0.500$. F1 $= 0.500$.

Macro average — every class gets one equal vote

$$\text{Macro}(M) = \frac{M_R + M_P + M_S}{3}$$
$$\text{Macro Precision} = \frac{0.857 + 0.571 + 0.500}{3} = \frac{1.928}{3} = \mathbf{0.643}$$ $$\text{Macro Recall} = \frac{0.750 + 0.667 + 0.500}{3} = \frac{1.917}{3} = \mathbf{0.639}$$ $$\text{Macro F1} = \frac{0.800 + 0.615 + 0.500}{3} = \frac{1.915}{3} = \mathbf{0.638}$$

Macro averaging class size ignore karta hai. Ise tab use karo jab ek chhoti class ko ek badi class jitna hi matter karna chahiye.

Weighted average — use actual class count as the weight

$$\text{Weighted}(M) = \frac{8M_R + 6M_P + 4M_S}{18}$$
$$\text{Weighted Precision} = \frac{8(6/7) + 6(4/7) + 4(1/2)}{18} = \frac{43}{63} \approx \mathbf{0.683}$$ $$\text{Weighted Recall} = \frac{8(0.750) + 6(0.667) + 4(0.500)}{18} = \frac{6 + 4 + 2}{18} = \frac{12}{18} \approx \mathbf{0.667}$$ $$\text{Weighted F1} = \frac{8(0.800) + 6(0.615) + 4(0.500)}{18} = \frac{6.40 + 3.69 + 2.00}{18} \approx \mathbf{0.672}$$

Micro average — pool all class decisions first

$TP = 12$, $FP = 6$, $FN = 6$ (pooled across all three classes).

$$\text{Micro Precision} = \frac{12}{12+6} = \frac{12}{18} = \mathbf{0.667}$$ $$\text{Micro Recall} = \frac{12}{12+6} = \frac{12}{18} = \mathbf{0.667}$$ $$\text{Micro F1} = \frac{2(12)}{2(12)+6+6} = \frac{24}{36} = \mathbf{0.667}$$

Har wrong single-label prediction predicted class ke liye ek pooled FP contribute karta hai aur missed actual class ke liye ek pooled FN. Isliye is task mein micro precision, recall aur F1 accuracy ke barabar ho jaate hain.

Why is weighted F1 (0.672) higher than macro F1 (0.638) here?

Kyunki sabse weak class bhi sabse chhoti class hai.

Needs Support ka F1 sabse kam (0.500) hai aur usme sirf 4 students hain. Macro use poora $1/3$ vote deta hai. Weighted use sirf $4/18 = 22\%$ weight deta hai, aur sabse strong class (Ready, F1 $=0.800$) ko $8/18 = 44\%$ weight deta hai. Weighting effectively model ki sabse badi weakness ko down-weight kar deti hai — isliye score upar aa jaata hai.

Practical lesson: agar minority class operationally important hai (jaise Needs Support yahan hai), to macro report karo — weighted ek rosier picture dikhayega jo asli problem chhupa deta hai.

△ DO NOT LET THIS MISCONCEPTION SURVIVE

Macro F1 class F1 scores ka arithmetic average hai. Yeh generally macro precision aur macro recall ka harmonic mean NAHI hai. (Check: harmonic mean of 0.643 and 0.639 = 0.641 ≠ 0.638.)

♦ Takeaway

Exit Check

Three questions — with full answers

  1. 1. How can two regression datasets have the same RMSE but different $R^2$ values?

    RMSE sirf $\text{RSS}/n$ par depend karta hai. $R^2 = 1 - \text{RSS}/\text{TSS}$ TSS par bhi depend karta hai — yaani target ka apne mean ke around spread. Same errors, par ek dataset mein targets tightly clustered hon (chhota TSS) to $R^2$ kam hoga, kyunki mean baseline ko beat karna mushkil hai. Spread-out targets (bada TSS) ke saath wahi errors high $R^2$ dete hain.

  2. 2. Why did Model B obtain a higher $R^2$ but a lower Adjusted $R^2$ than Model A?

    Model B ne RSS 74 se 60 kar di, to raw $R^2$ $0.9295 \to 0.9429$ badh gaya (raw $R^2$ predictor add karne par kabhi ghat nahi sakta). Par B ek teesra predictor use karta hai, to penalty factor $\frac{n-1}{n-p-1}$ $\frac{7}{5} = 1.4$ se $\frac{7}{4} = 1.75$ ho gaya — 25% badi penalty. Unexplained fraction sirf $0.0705 \to 0.0571$ (19% reduction) hui. 19% gain 25% penalty ko cover nahi kar paaya, to Adjusted $R^2$ $0.9013 \to 0.9000$ gir gaya.

  3. 3. How can the classifier be 66.7% accurate while recall for Needs Support is only 50%?

    Accuracy saare 18 students par compute hoti hai aur sabse badi class dominate karti hai. Ready ke 8 students mein se 6 correct hain — akele yeh $6/18 = 33\%$ accuracy contribute karte hain. Needs Support mein sirf 4 students hain; 2 miss karna accuracy se sirf $2/18 \approx 11\%$ cost karta hai, par us class ka recall aadha kar deta hai ($2/4 = 0.50$). Yehi wajah hai ki overall accuracy ke saath per-class recall bhi report karna zaroori hai.

⚡ Quick Revision

MAE

$\frac1n\sum|e_i| = 22/8 = 2.75$

MAPE

$\frac{100}{n}\sum\left|\frac{e_i}{y_i}\right| = 5.69\%$ · undefined at $y_i = 0$

MSE / RMSE

$74/8 = 9.25$ · $\sqrt{9.25} = 3.04$

$R^2$

$1 - \frac{\text{RSS}}{\text{TSS}} = 1 - \frac{74}{1050} = 0.9295$

Adjusted $R^2$

$1 - \left(\frac{\text{RSS}}{\text{TSS}}\right)\left(\frac{n-1}{n-p-1}\right)$

Accuracy

$\frac{\text{diagonal}}{\text{total}} = \frac{12}{18} = 0.667$

Precision / Recall

$\frac{TP}{TP+FP}$ / $\frac{TP}{TP+FN}$ · both 0.500 here

F1

$\frac{2TP}{2TP+FP+FN}$ · does not use TN

Macro vs Weighted vs Micro

Equal vote (0.638) · size-weighted (0.672) · pooled = accuracy (0.667)

FP vs FN

FP = Type I (false alert) hurts precision. FN = Type II (miss) hurts recall.
